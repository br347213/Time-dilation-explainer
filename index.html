But isn’t there one real “now” for both of us?

No. That is the everyday assumption relativity breaks. There is no universal master clock that every place in the universe shares. When two observers move differently, they can disagree about how much time lies between events and even which distant events count as “now.” When they reunite and put their clocks side by side, the different elapsed times are physical and measurable.

The picture to remember: same light speed + a longer moving path forces more time between moving ticks.
